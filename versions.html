Sep 24 – Nov 11 · EN-01 – EN-89

Wood robot

Our first robot: a 1/4 in plywood frame we cut on our laser cutter, with 3D-printed PLA parts. The plan was to pick up both ball sizes, sort them by size, and shoot each with its own hood shooter.

  • Sep–Oct, two shooters: we split plates to fit the laser, fixed the drive chain, found by video that the pollen shooter wasn't squeezing the ball, and bench tested a size sorter (Pollen 5/5 into the slot). The pollen shooter v4 was built and bench tested.
  • Nov 1–10, Pollen only: we had no reliable way to feed Nectar, so we chose a Pollen-only robot for the qualifier (EN-59), added a flower wedge, and stiffened the drive pods.

Why we moved on: in class footage, the shots that reached the hive bounced back out (3 of 3 we could follow). The hood shooter gives the ball backspin. EN-74

Nov 11 – 16 · EN-90 – EN-103

CR1

CR1 ("concept robot 1") is a new robot built around a topspin shooter: the wheel is on top of the ball and a grippy ramp is under it, so the ball spins forward and rolls into the hive instead of bouncing out. Another FTC team had found the same thing while prototyping.

  • We kept the drivetrain, odometry, hubs and wedge, and added a turret (a lazy susan turned by two servos, ±90°), a 4-ball lane and a transfer that squeezes the ball up into the head.
  • Before building the robot, we drew test rigs for each new part: the shooter head, the turret, the front module and the wedge.
  • The topspin head on the turret rig shot much more consistently, so we made it one printed piece. EN-102

Nov 16 – 19 · EN-104 – EN-110

CR1 v6

CR1 v6 is the clean-up of CR1 in six planned steps. The goal: fewer parts, parts that fit our machines, and room to spare inside the 18 in box.

  • One bracket shape instead of four; one printed sign instead of 16 parts; one idler on the transfer chain instead of three.
  • Full-length side plates cut in one piece on a bigger laser (Glowforge).
  • The robot had only 0.35 mm to spare on each side. We moved the pods 5 mm in for 0.25 in of margin. EN-108
  • We tried putting the wedge arms inside the pods so other robots can't break them, but chose to keep them outside. EN-109

Nov 19 – now · EN-111 – EN-115

v6 METAL

A sponsor gave us a $500 credit for metal parts. v6 METAL is the same robot with its 7 most important plates in 1/8 in aluminum: the side plates that hold the wheels, the drive motor plates and the rear panel.

  • Every plate keeps the face that sets where parts go, so no wheel, chain, motor or turret moves. Printed parts grow 3.2 mm to fill the space. EN-111
  • The lane became two 3D prints instead of 7 parts, with 3 small extra wheels so a ball never sits still. EN-112 · EN-113
  • The camera moved to the top of the head so the wedge never blocks its view, and we are testing a lower wedge for the flower. EN-114 · EN-115

Status: Files ready The whole robot is designed and checked in CAD. Next: cut, print, build and test before the qualifier on December 20.
